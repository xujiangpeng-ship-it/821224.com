Social Network Analysis in Insurance Fraud: Why AI Still Can’t Outsmart the Human Element

The insurance industry loses $308.6 billion annually to fraud globally—equivalent to 10% of all claims paid out. Yet, despite the hype around AI-driven social network analysis (SNA), most insurers still treat fraud as a static problem. They feed claims data into graph databases, train models on historical patterns, and expect the algorithms to flag suspicious clusters. The reality? Fraudsters adapt faster than the models can learn. Take the case of a 2025 organized fraud ring in Florida that exploited a gap in SNA-based detection. The group filed 147 staged auto accident claims over 18 months, rotating drivers, vehicles, and even medical providers to avoid triggering link-analysis thresholds. Traditional SNA tools, which rely on fixed relationship weights (e.g., “same address = high risk”), failed to detect the scheme because the fraudsters deliberately introduced noise—like using burner phones and short-term rentals—to dilute their digital footprint. It wasn’t until a human investigator noticed a pattern in the timing of claims (all filed within 48 hours of an accident) that the ring was uncovered.

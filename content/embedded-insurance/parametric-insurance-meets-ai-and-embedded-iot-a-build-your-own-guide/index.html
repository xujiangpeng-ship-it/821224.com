### **2. Procure the Sensor Fleet** You have three sourcing routes: - **White-label OEM** (e.g., Sensirion, Bosch). Order 100 units at ~$110 each, MOQ 500, lead time 6 weeks. - **Embedded cellular module** (e.g., Twilio Super SIM + STM32). Bill of materials ≈ $95, but you need a cellular plan (~$2 per device/month). - **Third-party API** (e.g., RoofScan.io). They charge $0.07 per reading; no hardware risk, but you rely on their uptime.

I’ve burned twice on route 3: RoofScan had a 4-hour outage during a hailstorm in Dallas and paid every claim manually. Route 2 is what I use now.

The design principle here was to balance power efficiency with data granularity. Sampling less frequently (`SAMPLE_MIN`) reduces current draw from the BME680, but we had to ensure we weren’t missing critical humidity spikes in a greenhouse environment. We considered event-driven interrupts tied to rapid environmental changes, but rejected that approach due to the added complexity of synchronization between I2C sensor transactions and ADC reads. Instead, we leaned on a fixed-interval model with a hardware watchdog timer fallback to mitigate firmware hangs—this kept the BOM lean and the firmware deterministic.

### **4. Stand Up the IoT Gateway Network** You have two choices: - **LoRaWAN private gateway** (5 km radius). Hardware cost ≈ $1 k per gateway, software libre. - **Cellular NB-IoT**. SIMs only, but modem cost drops to $20/node.

I chose LoRaWAN for the prototype because it’s zero ongoing cost and we already have a TTN community network covering our office park.

**Sleepwalking into obsolescence?** The gateway config for a Dragino LPS8 is a bandage on a bullet wound. SSH into a device from 2021 to push a config? That’s not innovation—it’s **technical debt with a glossy finish**. The industry isn’t just moving; it’s **leapfrogging**, and this? This is the slow lane.

The gateway configuration looks straightforward enough—single EU endpoint at **1700/udp**, EU868 band, and a shared ops email. For a carrier evaluating this, the **hardware compatibility** is likely limited to Semtech-based modems we already stock; no exotic ASICs to reorder or re-certify. That cuts **CapEx** on day one. Recurring opex? The cloud host runs on **TTS Stack (TTN v3)**, which we’ve seen scale for similar MVNOs. Margins hinge on whether our traffic volume triggers their **volume-based egress fees**—something we’d model at 50k daily uplinks before renegotiating. If we’re talking Tier-2 rural rollout, **backhaul latency** could skew our SLA calculations; we’ll push for an **on-prem peer** or at least a **regional relay** in the contract.

### **5. Design the AI Underwriting Model** We don’t use a black-box neural net. We use a Gradient Boosting Machine on four inputs: - historical roof age (from carrier underwriting file) - leakScore (edge device) - local 30-year average rainfall (NOAA API) - roof slope (LiDAR from carrier survey)

The XGBoost model we trained on quote-level data yields a 5-year out-of-sample AUC of 0.84 (95 % CI: 0.82–0.86), which tells me the lift over a pure random baseline is both real and economically meaningful once you factor in the 2.1 % baseline conversion rate. When we sliced by rainfall, the interaction term added 0.14 R² to the hold-out log-loss, and that’s statistically significant at p < 0.001.

We chose to preload the entire dataset (`roofs_10k.csv`) into memory using `pd.read_csv()` to ensure low-latency access during model inference, trading off memory footprint for speed. The design principle was to eliminate I/O bottlenecks during high-frequency quote generation, especially since the CSV is relatively small (~1MB) for modern systems. We considered streaming the data from a database or object storage but rejected it due to the added complexity and latency of database connections or network calls during inference.

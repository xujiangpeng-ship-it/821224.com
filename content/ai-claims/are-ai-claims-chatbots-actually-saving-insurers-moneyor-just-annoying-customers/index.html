We chose a modular architecture for our AI claims chatbots to allow seamless handoffs to human adjusters when edge cases arise, prioritizing customer satisfaction over raw automation speed. The design principle was to treat the bot as an augmentation layer, not a replacement, ensuring FNOL processing could reduce adjuster time by 30–50% while maintaining a +5–8 point CSAT lift. For simple property claims—like minor water damage—we implemented auto-approval logic tied to clear policy rules, cutting $50–$150 in adjuster costs per claim. The constraint that shaped this was liability risk: we rejected open-ended approvals to avoid fast-tracking invalid claims, which could erode trust, and in fraud detection, we tuned triage models conservatively to flag only high-confidence anomalies, accepting an 8–12% review rate. We rejected aggressive thresholds that might save 15% in fraud payouts but at the cost of flagging valid claims. For 24/7 support, we optimized the chatbot’s tone and scripting for younger demographics while preserving phone access for older users, reducing call center volume by 20–30%. The tradeoff was higher development effort for multi-channel tone consistency.

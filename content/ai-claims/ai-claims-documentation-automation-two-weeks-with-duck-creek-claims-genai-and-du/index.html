### **The Human Cost Behind the Efficiency Gains: Who Bears the Burden of AI in Claims?**

While the promise of AI-driven claims processing is efficiency and speed, the reality for many policyholders—especially those in marginalized or non-English-speaking communities—is far more complicated. Automated systems like Duck Creek’s GenAI and Claims Lens AI are trained on datasets that reflect existing biases in healthcare, medical coding, and even everyday language use. For example, research shows that **speech recognition models struggle with non-standard accents and dialects**, often misclassifying or underrepresenting claims from Black, Latino, or elderly patients. When an AI system consistently misidentifies medical terms due to dialect drift or poor training data, it doesn’t just slow down processing—it can **deny legitimate claims** based on flawed interpretations. The NAIC’s 2023-1 bias audit highlighted an **8.3% error rate for Spanish FNOL calls**, which is not just a technical flaw but a **real-world barrier** to fair treatment for millions of policyholders who rely on non-English support. Without robust safeguards—such as **mandatory dialect-specific fine-tuning, diverse dataset inclusion, and third-party bias audits**—these tools risk perpetuating systemic inequities under the guise of modernization.

Transparency is another critical issue. Most policyholders have no idea how AI influences their claim outcome—whether it’s an automatic denial, a delayed payment, or a settlement offer skewed by an algorithm. The **lack of explainability** in AI-driven decisions means consumers are left in the dark, unable to challenge or even understand why their claim was handled a certain way. This opacity is particularly harmful in **bodily injury (BI) claims**, where nuanced medical details and subjective pain assessments are crucial. The model’s **12.5% false-negative rate on subtle fractures** (as cited in the memo) isn’t just a statistical footnote—it represents real people waiting longer for care, facing financial strain, or being wrongly told their injuries aren’t severe enough to warrant compensation. Consumers need **clear, accessible explanations** of how AI affects their claims, along with **human review options** when automated decisions feel unjust. Without these safeguards, we risk turning claims processing into a **black-box system** where technology, not fairness, dictates outcomes.

### **Technical Architecture Deep Dive: Model Design, Training, and Operational Constraints**

The GenAI pipeline is built on a **fine-tuned Azure OpenAI gpt-4-0613**, deployed as a regulated microservice on Kubernetes (AKS) with GPU-accelerated inference to handle transcription and summarization. The model was pre-trained on 12,000 anonymized FNOL calls from Duck Creek clients, using a **hybrid transformer-CRF architecture** optimized for named entity recognition (NER) in medical and automotive contexts. The summarization module employs **longformer-style attention** with a 4K-token window to preserve narrative coherence despite background noise. A secondary **BERT-based classifier** (fine-tuned on 50,000 claim notes) routes claims into simple, moderate, or complex tiers—with a deterministic fallback for BI claims, given the model’s 12.5% false-negative rate on subtle fractures (per Chen et al., 2024). Lens, by contrast, uses a **graph neural network (GNN) over medical bills** (UB-04/CMS-1500 forms) to extract ICD-10 and CPT codes, achieving 96% accuracy on clean TIFF inputs. While the 12,000-call corpus includes modern vernacular (e.g., “morphine 5mg”), clinical language evolves rapidly—drug names, treatment protocols, and patient phrasing drift at **15–20% per 18 months** (Pham et al., 2023). Duck Creek does not expose a continuous-learning API; recalibration requires full model retraining, which vendors quote at **6–8 weeks** with a 20% uplift in license fees. The NAIC 2023-1 bulletin mandates **quarterly bias audits**, but the model’s **regional dialect sensitivity** (e.g., 8.3% error rate on Spanish FNOL calls vs. 4.1% for English) reveals a gap between compliance rhetoric and operational practice. Without **proactive dialect-specific fine-tuning** or **adversarial debiasing**, the model risks disparate impact—especially in no-fault states where multilingual claimants are overrepresented.

While Duck Creek’s benchmarking highlights productivity gains, does the **license fee per adjuster** justify the **10.5 hours saved monthly** at current claim volumes? - **Integration Complexity:** A two-week pilot is insufficient for assessing **full-scale system integration.** We need clarity on: - **API compatibility** with our existing P/CMS (e.g., Guidewire, Duck Creek’s own platform). - **Data migration effort**—will historical claim notes require manual tagging for AI training? - **Adjuster training overhead**—how steep is the learning curve, and what’s the **lost productivity during adoption?** - **Vendor Lock-in Risk:** If Duck Creek’s AI stack is proprietary, future transitions (e.g., switching core systems) could incur **unforeseen migration costs.** We must demand **portable data formats** and **open API assurances.** - **Time-to-Value (TTV):** The pilot’s “uneven results” suggest **variable accuracy by claim type.** Before committing, we need: - **Controlled A/B testing** across our full claim portfolio (not just FNOL). - **Performance benchmarks** (e.g., 95%+ accuracy for BI claims) with **financial penalties for failures.** - **What the Reference Call Actually Revealed:** The mid-market carrier’s 55% BI frequency may not reflect our book.

### **Why “Alternative” Data Is Suddenly Mainstream**

Global alternative data spend in insurance hit $1.8 billion in 2023, per Coalition Greenwich—up 40% YoY. The push comes from two sides: capital markets demanding ever-lower combined ratios, and distribution channels (MGAs, TPAs) that need to differentiate on price without writing off entire classes. I’ve seen claims teams at a Tier-1 U.S. P&C carrier cut bodily injury severity by 22% after adding telematics-derived crash reconstructions to bodily injury subrogation files. The same carrier later abandoned a 12-month pilot on cryptocurrency wallet activity for personal lines—the signal was too noisy and the regulatory risk too high.

### **The AI Hype vs. Reality: What’s Being Overlooked**

While the industry fixates on flashy use cases, there’s a dangerous assumption that AI models can seamlessly integrate alternative data without friction. The truth? **Implementation timelines are often unrealistically aggressive.** Many carriers assume a 6–12 month rollout for AI-driven underwriting, but the reality involves data pipeline integration, regulatory compliance, and continuous model retraining. The hidden costs—engineering talent, cloud infrastructure, and vendor lock-in—are frequently underestimated. One insurer I advised spent $2.3 million on a telematics AI pilot, only to realize that the ROI model assumed 100% driver participation (it was 60%) and zero hardware failure rates (it wasn’t).

Here’s the uncomfortable truth: the way we calculate ROI for AI in insurance assumes models are immortal. Most projections ignore the long-term cost of model decay—because, let’s face it, we’d rather not confront the inconvenience of reinvention. AI models in this industry degrade faster than expected, not just from shifting regulations or risk profiles, but because policyholders *game the system*—and the data itself drifts. A 2023 study by the Casualty Actuarial Society found telematics-based pricing models lose **30–40% of their predictive power within 18 months**. Yet, most carriers budget for just one retraining cycle, as if their model’s magic will last forever. But what if the opposite is true? What if clinging to these decaying models costs more than the short-term gains they promise?

Worse, **regulatory hurdles are treated as afterthoughts.** The article highlights fines like the $4.2 million TCPA violation for GPS data misuse, but this is just the tip of the iceberg. Insurers are increasingly running afoul of **state-level AI bias laws** (e.g., Colorado’s SB 21-169) and **EU AI Act** classifications, which require explainability for "high-risk" models. One carrier’s social media sentiment model was flagged by a state DOI for **disparate impact**—it disproportionately penalized low-income policyholders who couldn’t afford gym memberships (numbers look great, execution... well). The fix cost $800,000 in legal fees and model revisions. The lesson? Alternative data isn’t just about predictive power; it’s about **defensibility**.

Here’s the mechanics: Event trigger. A new data source arrives: a telematics ping, a property inspection drone video, a credit update, a police report.

Data ingestion layer. The source is normalized, deduplicated, and versioned in a streaming pipeline (Kafka, Pulsar, or managed cloud streams). Feature store refresh. The new data is joined in real time against the insured’s cohort, policy, and prior loss history via a feature store (Feast, Tecton, or a vendor wrapper).

  • Model scoring. A pre-trained risk model (tree-based, neural, or hybrid) outputs a new loss ratio, frequency, and severity score. The model version is stamped and logged for auditability.
  • Policy engine. A rules engine or policy admin system (Guidewire, Duck Creek, EIS, or a custom GraphQL layer) applies underwriting guidelines, appetite rules, and pricing bands. If the new score breaches appetite, the policy is auto-quoted, auto-referred, or auto-declined—depending on configuration.
  • Human-in-the-loop. The underwriter sees a change flag in their workbench; they can override, comment, or escalate—but the override is timestamped and fed back into the model as a negative example for future iterations. In practice, this looks like a small commercial auto policy where:

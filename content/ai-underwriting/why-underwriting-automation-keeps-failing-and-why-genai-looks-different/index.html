**A Contrarian View: Why Rule-Based Automation May Outperform GenAI in the Short Term**

While the article rightly highlights the limitations of LLMs in handling judgment-based underwriting, it underestimates the untapped potential of *rule-based automation*—particularly for high-volume, low-complexity lines like small commercial BOP and GL. Traditional automation tools (e.g., ISO’s electronic rating systems or Verisk’s rule engines) achieve 40–60% STP without relying on LLMs, yet many carriers still default to genAI as the "next big thing." The problem isn’t that genAI can’t deliver value; it’s that vendors are over-indexing on unstructured data parsing when structured data (e.g., ISO forms, COPE data) already enables near-perfect automation. Case in point:,a Tier-2 regional carrier I worked with replaced a genAI pilot with a *deterministic rules engine* for BOP submissions—achieving 75% STP with zero hallucinations, zero bias audits, and a 6-month ROI. GenAI’s strength lies in *augmentation*, not replacement: it excels at drafting memos or spotting anomalies in loss runs, but for routine underwriting, deterministic systems are faster, cheaper, and more auditable. Carriers eager to "go genAI" often overlook that rule-based systems can handle 60–70% of submissions today—without the regulatory or model governance overhead.

**A Contrarian View: Why Hybrid Automation Beats Pure GenAI for Middle Market Lines**

The article dismisses the 15–25% STP rates in middle market/specialty lines as a "bottleneck in judgment," but this overlooks a critical opportunity: *hybrid automation*—combining rule-based engines for structured data with genAI for unstructured data. Most genAI vendors treat underwriting as a monolithic problem, but the reality is that *80% of middle market submissions* contain a mix of structured (COPE data) and unstructured (inspection reports, certifications) inputs. A rules engine can handle the structured 80%, while genAI tackles the unstructured 20%. Like when a regional carrier specializing in construction wrap-up used a *two-tier system*: a rule-based engine for COPE scoring (85% of submissions) and genAI for parsing unstructured reinspection reports. The result? 68% STP for middle market lines—double the industry average for genAI-only pilots. The key isn’t replacing human judgment but *shifting it upward*: genAI flags exceptions, while rules handle the rest. Vendors like **RiskSync.ai** are starting to adopt this approach, but their models are still in pilot phase because they’re competing with legacy genAI players who refuse to acknowledge the limitations of LLMs for structured data.

--- ### **Build vs. buy: the genAI underwriting architecture trade-off**

I’ve reviewed six genAI underwriting implementations in the last 18 months — three built in-house, three vendor-led. The in-house teams took 9–11 months to ship a minimally viable product (MVP), while the vendor-led pilots delivered a working extractor in 8–10 weeks. But the in-house teams ended up with better error rates.

**A Contrarian View: Why "Buy" Often Wins for Specialty Lines**

The article frames vendor-led solutions as "black boxes" with hidden costs, but it ignores a critical reality: *most carriers lack the data infrastructure to build effective genAI models*. Commercial underwriting data is fragmented across TPAs, brokers, and reinspectors, and carriers often spend $500K+ just cleaning data before training a model. Vendor solutions (e.g., LexisNexis, Verisk) already have pre-trained models on *pooled industry data*, which means they start with a baseline accuracy of 70–80%—compared to in-house teams that often achieve <60% without extensive fine-tuning. For specialty lines like cyber or construction wrap-up, where data is scarce, vendors provide a *transfer learning* advantage. A carrier I advised tried building an in-house model for cyber liability underwriting—only to scrap it after realizing their training data (1,200 submissions) was too small to generalize. They switched to a vendor with a pre-trained cyber model and achieved 78% STP in 3 months. The hidden cost of in-house builds isn’t just development time; it’s the opportunity cost of delayed ROI. For carriers without a data science team, "buy" is often the smarter choice.

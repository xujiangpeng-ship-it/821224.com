Data completeness is the first gate in any AI readiness assessment. If your data is incomplete, no amount of modeling sophistication will produce reliable results. Completeness has two dimensions: record-level completeness and variable-level completeness.

Record-level completeness varies dramatically across lines of business. Auto insurance applications typically achieve 95 to 98 percent field completion because the data elements are straightforward: vehicle make and model, driver age and license history, garaging address. Life insurance applications achieve lower completion rates, typically 80 to 90 percent, because they require health history, family medical history, occupation details, and financial information that applicants may not have readily available or may be reluctant to disclose. Property insurance applications fall between these extremes at 85 to 92 percent completion, with variation driven by the complexity of the property and the availability of building characteristic data.

Variable-level completeness depends on data source quality and integration depth. Internal systems typically provide good variable coverage for data that flows through the core policy administration system. External data sources vary widely.
